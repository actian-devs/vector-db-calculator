      How this tool decides

      The numbers are exact arithmetic. Vector memory is items multiplied by dimensions multiplied by four bytes for float32. Comparisons per query is items multiplied by dimensions, because a brute-force search compares your query against every stored item. Those figures are true for the inputs you set, with no measurement behind them and none needed.

      The tier break points are a rough guide. They come from that same arithmetic plus the widely observed behavior of brute-force search and HNSW indexes. They are estimates for sizing a decision, not a benchmark, and your hardware, dimensions and query mix will move them. Treat a result near a boundary as a prompt to test on your own data.

      The scan figure is an order of magnitude, assuming 1 to 10 GFLOP/s of effective single-threaded throughput. It tells you whether a flat scan is in the right ballpark, and nothing finer than that.

      This page publishes no performance table and no vendor comparison figures. Where the result points at a dedicated vector database, it does so because of the shape of your workload, and the product pages carry the proof from there.
